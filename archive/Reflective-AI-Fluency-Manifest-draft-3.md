# Reflective AI Fluency: Manifest (draft 3)

Nov 4, 2026 · Sebastian Nicolajsen

Reflective AI Fluency is the ability to work with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically and safely in your own work, and to keep getting better at it. It builds on the 4D AI Fluency Framework by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence) and adds what that framework leaves implicit: practising it in one's own context, choosing deliberately how to work with AI on each step, and the reflective quality that makes practice compound.

The framework has two axes. **Ways of working** describe who sets the path of the work, from a single interaction to agentic work. **Quality** describes how well and how reflectively the four competencies are performed. The two are linked: the more autonomy and the higher the stakes, the higher the quality needed to work safely.

## What we believe

Fluency is something you do, in your own work, and it shows in how you adapt. Nine statements set the stance.

1. **Fluency is behaviour, not knowledge.** Knowing the 4Ds matters only if it changes what people do with AI on a Tuesday afternoon.
2. **Fluency lives in context.** It is learned on your own tasks, in your own domain, because that is where you can tell a good answer from a wrong one.
3. **Judgement comes before speed.** Checking, questioning and noticing what is missing are the habits most people skip, so they come first.
4. **Polish is not proof.** The more finished AI output looks, the more deliberately it must be checked.
5. **No way of working is the goal.** Single interaction, collaboration, handover and agentic work are all tools. Fluency is choosing the right one for each step and changing when it stops working. Often the right choice is the simplest one.
6. **Autonomy grows with judgement.** People hand AI more of the path only as their ability to judge the result keeps up.
7. **Fluent people get better on their own.** They look for what they are missing, notice, adjust and carry lessons forward; that quality is what separates fluency from competence.
8. **Some work stays human on purpose.** Fluency includes knowing what you should keep doing yourself, to protect judgement, expertise or responsibility.
9. **Good enough is the default trap.** AI answers anything fluently and rarely shows you what you missed, so people settle for the first acceptable result. Fluent people go looking for the better one.

## The four competencies

We keep Dakan and Feller's four competencies unchanged and express each as a question a person asks about their own work.

| Competency | In plain words | The question in your own work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delegation | Deciding whether, when and how to use AI, and in which way of working | Should AI help with this step, and how much should it do? |
| Description | Communicating goals, context, format and how the AI should work | Have I told it what I would tell a capable new colleague? |
| Discernment | Judging output, reasoning and the collaboration itself | How would I know if this is wrong, incomplete or misleading? |
| Diligence | Taking responsibility: choosing tools well, being open about AI's role, verifying before use | Can I stand behind this, and would I be comfortable saying how it was made? |

All four apply in every way of working; what changes is how they are performed.

## Axis 1: Ways of working

The four ways are sorted by **who determines the path of the work**.

| Way of working | Who sets the path | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single interaction | You, in one step | One question or instruction, one response, judged by you |
| Collaboration | You and the AI, step by step | Iterating together; the path emerges as you go |
| Handover | You, in advance | You define the steps, inputs and checks; the AI executes them, often repeatedly |
| Agentic | The AI, within boundaries | You set goals, boundaries and checkpoints; the AI chooses its own path |

**Relation to Dakan and Feller's modes.** Collaboration corresponds to their Augmentation and Agentic to their Agency. Their Automation (AI performs a specific task on your instruction) is split in two: a single interaction and a designed, repeatable handover. We depart from the source here because the difference in who sets the path changes what Discernment and Diligence must do.

**No ladder.** The ways differ in difficulty and in the quality they require, not in value. A single interaction is often exactly right. The choice is made per step, so one person uses all four in a day, and some steps use none.

### Choosing the way of working

Decide per step. Autonomy is capped by how well you can check the result and what an undetected error would cost.

| Question | If no |
| --- | --- |
| Is the goal clear? | Collaboration: explore with AI to find what good looks like |
| Can I check the output, quickly or at checkpoints? | Collaboration, or keep the step human |
| What does an undetected error cost, and is it reversible? | If high and irreversible: AI drafts, human decides |
| Is one exchange enough? | Collaboration, if the work needs several rounds |
| Do I know the path in advance? | Not yet: collaboration or agentic, with boundaries and checkpoints. Yes: handover |
| Does it recur or have volume? | Don't invest in building a handover; a one-off run is fine if the first three answers are yes |

Recurrence decides whether building a handover is worth the effort. It does not decide whether delegating is allowed.

**Step-down triggers:** output drifts, stakes rise, or you can no longer say how you would know it is wrong.

The common mistake is starting with the most autonomous way before you know what good looks like. Exploring a new way is safest on a step from your own domain, where you can still judge the result.

### Each competency, anchored per way of working

| | Single interaction | Collaboration | Handover | Agentic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delegation | Is this worth asking AI? | Which parts does AI help with, and how much? | Which steps go to AI, which stay human? | What may the AI decide, within which boundaries? |
| Description | A question with enough context to be answerable | Goal, context and format, refined as you go | A specification of the path, inputs and expected output | A brief with goals, boundaries and checkpoints |
| Discernment | Notices obvious errors; checks a claim that matters | Questions reasoning, spots gaps, judges whether the collaboration works | Places review points in the process | Judges behaviour and drift across runs, not single outputs |
| Diligence | Does not share sensitive data | Verifies before sharing; is open about AI's role | Builds verification and disclosure into the process | Answers for outputs that others rely on |

## Axis 2: Quality

Quality is how well and how reflectively the four competencies are performed in a given way of working. It is assessed within a way of working, not across them.

### Reflective quality

Reflection is not a fifth competency or a separate loop; it is the quality with which all four are performed. A fluent person works like a fluent speaker: they notice a misunderstanding and rephrase mid-sentence, without stopping to run a procedure.

It shows in five ways:

- **Curiosity.** Looking for what a better result would be, even when the current one seems fine: asking for alternatives, comparing approaches, trying another way of working.
- **Noticing.** Recognising, during the work, that output is drifting, a check is missing or the way of working is wrong.
- **Reviewing.** Looking back after the work: what did I just do, what worked, what would I change?
- **Adjusting.** Changing the description, the check or the way of working in response, including trying a different way on a task you already know well.
- **Carrying forward.** Turning what worked into reusable practice (a prompt, a checklist, a handover) and revising it when it fails.

Noticing happens during the work; reviewing happens after it. While fluency is developing, both are often deliberate and visible; with fluency they become part of how the work is done.

Its opposite is **satisficing**: stopping at the first result that is good enough. With AI this is the default, not a character flaw. The output always looks acceptable, the model rarely signals what is missing, and good-enough use already saves time, so there is no felt reason to improve. The cost is real but invisible: errors no one sees and value no one reaches. Curiosity is the counterweight.

### Observable traces of reflection

Reflection cannot be seen in a snapshot. It is a difference between two points in time on the same task. We look for these traces:

- A prompt, check or handover that changed between versions, with a stated reason.
- A check added after an error was caught.
- A way of working switched when output drifted or stakes rose.
- Alternatives requested after an acceptable answer (the anti-satisficing trace).
- A reusable artifact revised after it failed.
- The person can name what the AI got wrong last time and what they do differently now.

**Method:** compare two runs of the same task and ask one question: "What did you change since last time, and why?"

**Rule:** no change and no reason means no evidence, not failure. A stable practice that still works is not a lack of reflection.

### Dimensions of quality (proposed)

Quality is described as dimensions that run from weaker to stronger, not as levels. They apply within each way of working.

| Dimension | Weaker | Stronger |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Failures caught | Errors found by others later, or never | Errors found by you, during the work |
| Check fit | The same check whatever the risk, or none | The check matches what an error would cost |
| Reasons | Cannot say why this way, check or prompt | Can say why, and what would change the choice |
| Adaptivity | Same practice whatever the task or output | Practice changes when the task or output changes |
| Transfer | What was learned stays with one task | What was learned carries to new tasks and ways of working |

*Status: proposed. "Failures caught" is grounded in research on visible and invisible AI failures (Potts & Sudhof); the others are our derivation and need testing on real work.*

### How the axes link

- **Required quality rises with autonomy and with stakes.** Autonomy decides how late you would see an error: in collaboration you catch it as it happens, in agentic work only at checkpoints. Stakes decide what an undetected error costs. The same weak check that is harmless in a single interaction is dangerous in a handover others rely on.
- **Curiosity pushes, Discernment permits.** Curiosity drives people toward more autonomous ways of working. Discernment decides whether the move is safe. Curiosity without the ability to judge output only widens the scope of unchecked work.
- **Stepping down is quality.** Being able to run an agent is a way of working. Knowing when to step back from it is quality.

The axes give four positions, and the top right is the main risk:

| | Lower autonomy (single interaction, collaboration) | Higher autonomy (handover, agentic) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lower quality** | One-shot answers, accepted unchecked | Handovers or agents running with no real checks |
| **Higher quality** | Sharp interrogation of answers and iterations | Autonomy matched by checks, boundaries and revision |

## The behaviours we look for

We use Anthropic's behavioural indicators as the core (24 in total) and apply the reflective quality as a lens across them. Eleven can be seen in AI conversations; thirteen happen outside them.

**Observable in conversation:**

| Behaviour | Competency |
| --- | --- |
| Iterates and refines | Description |
| Clarifies the goal before asking for help | Delegation |
| Provides examples of what good looks like | Description |
| Specifies format and structure | Description |
| Sets how the AI should work (interaction mode) | Description |
| Communicates tone and style | Description |
| Identifies when AI might be missing context | Discernment |
| Defines the audience for the output | Description |
| Questions reasoning that doesn't hold up | Discernment |
| Consults AI on approach before execution | Delegation |
| Checks facts and claims that matter | Discernment |

**Outside the conversation** (13 indicators; wording below is a grouping, not Anthropic's exact text):

- **Delegation:** deciding whether AI should be used at all; choosing a suitable tool and way of working; splitting work between person and AI.
- **Discernment:** judging whether the collaboration is working; judging the AI's behaviour, not only its output.
- **Diligence:** considering privacy, bias and ethics before use; being open about AI's role; verifying before sharing; owning the consequences.

We also watch for the opposite behaviours: satisficing (stopping at the first acceptable output on work that matters), accepting output passively, delegating vaguely, and trusting polished output without checking.

## Fluency in your own work

Fluency is always fluency in something: it shows in how a person handles the steps of their own work.

For any step of their work, a fluent person can answer four questions:

| Question | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| Which way of working, or no AI, and why | Delegation |
| How I would know the output is wrong | Discernment |
| What role AI plays in the result (consultant, tool builder, cocreator, medium) | Diligence and openness about AI's role |
| Whether this step should stay human on purpose | Protecting expertise and judgement |

Over time, the answers change: new ways of working, new checks, steps joined into larger pieces of work. That change is the trace of growing fluency.

## What this is not

Reflective AI Fluency is not a tool course, not a prompt-technique catalogue and not a ranking of people. It does not treat automation, autonomy or any particular way of working as the goal, and it does not certify knowledge about AI.
