**Review of the interactive map design.** I assumed the audience is colleagues with mixed AI experience who come to look around. I read `DESIGN.md` and `content.js`. I could not open `source/manifest.md`, so none of the 18 links is checked against it. I also have not seen the page render. My judgements on alignment are tentative.

**1. The outcome contradicts the page's own stance.**
- The overview says: "After exploring, you can say, for a step of your own work, which way of working fits it, what checking it needs, and what would make you change your mind."
- The design table says: "It makes no claim that visiting changes how anyone works."
- Nothing on the page makes a visitor do those three things, and the design admits it leaves out practice, debriefs and outside feedback. A promise the page can't keep misleads the visitor about what they will get.
- Confidence: our judgement.

**2. "Choosing per step" reads as the top of a ladder.**
- It sits in the `way` cluster at `pos: [3.2, …]`, past Agency on the "who sets the path" axis. Your design says "no ladder", but this placement suggests one.
- It is not a way of working. It is the act of choosing between them, so it also has no "Fits when" or "Does not fit when". That breaks "Each way of working has…".
- The link `["choosing", "stakes", "raises", …]` points the wrong way. Its sentence says the choice "starts from what an undetected error would cost", so stakes feed the choice. As written, the arrow says choosing raises stakes.
- Fix: take it off the axis, for example as a frame around the five ways, and reverse the link.
- Confidence: plausible. The link direction is a plain reading of your own sentence.

**3. Several claims in the design don't match the content.**
- "4 to 5 items per part": quality has 7 concepts and the ways cluster has 6.
- "Foundations" holds Traces and Tools for looking back. Those are about looking back at your practice, not "what everything else stands on".
- 14 of 22 concepts are rated "our judgement", including all the ways of working. Their "Fits when" text comes from a choosing table that has not been tested on real work-process maps. Say so on the page, so colleagues don't read those entries as settled.

**Smaller points**
- The step field only echoes the visitor's text next to the question. It does not ask for an answer. Colleagues may type client details, so add a "don't enter anything confidential" line even though nothing is stored.
- `ENTRIES` says "agent" and the node is "Agency". Use one term.
- "Suggestions disappear for concepts already opened" is described as withdrawing support as skill grows. It only tracks what was clicked, so don't describe it as the rule.

**One specific strength:** "Connections are few and typed", each with a one-sentence reason. That is easy to check and to challenge.

**Decision for you:** for point 1, should the outcome become a plain orientation ("find the part of the framework that bears on a step of your work"), or do you want to keep the behavioural outcome and add a small "try it on your step" block that links to a separate journey?
