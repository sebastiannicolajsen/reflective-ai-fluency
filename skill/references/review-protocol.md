# Review protocol

Use this when someone shares a learning goal, exercise, session plan, journey, rubric or evaluation plan and wants a view, and as the skeleton for building one with them.

## Before judging

Ask at most two questions, then give something of value. Pick the two that most change the review:

1. What should people do differently afterwards, on which real step, in which way of working?
2. Who is it for, and how experienced are they with AI and with this way of working?

If the answer is not in the text, state your assumption and carry on. Also adjust to the designer: with a new designer, offer more structure and explain terms; with an experienced facilitator, be compact.

AI reviews of learning designs tend to over-praise and agree with experts only moderately. Treat your own judgements on alignment and depth as tentative, and say so.

## The review

1. **Lead with the two or three points that most change the design.** Not a full checklist.
2. **Quote the text** each point reacts to.
3. **Give the plain reason** with each point, as what goes wrong without the fix, using a concrete example from their design. Explain any framework term in the same sentence.
4. **Label the confidence:** established, plausible, preprint, or our judgement. Bring in the evidence behind a rule only when the designer challenges it, asks why, or works at expert level. See [arguments.md](arguments.md).
5. **Say what you could not check,** for example whether the task can be completed by AI alone, if you did not run it.
6. **Name one strength only if it is specific.** No total score, no default praise.
7. **Show the fix, not only the fault.** For each point, give a rewritten line or a sketched exercise that the designer can use or change. See the sketch in [teaching-notes.md](teaching-notes.md).
8. **End with a decision** the designer makes: a question or a choice between two fixes.

## Common first findings

These come up often. Check for them first, and report only those that apply.

- The outcome says "understand" or "be aware". Rewrite it as a behaviour in a named way of working, including the choice of no AI.
- The exercise can be finished by AI alone, so finishing proves nothing about the person's judgement. Ask for evidence that AI cannot produce alone: two runs with a reason for the change, or a comparison of AI output with the person's own view.
- Practice uses an invented or unfamiliar case. Use a task from participants' own work, in a domain they know.
- Nothing practises checking or judging output. Add a step where people meet an AI error and find it.
- No debrief that names one change for next time.
- No plan for the first real use at work, and no return to the same task type after a gap.
- Evaluation rests on a satisfaction survey or a knowledge test. Look for traces of changed work over time.

## Building with someone

When there is nothing to review yet, work through the layers in order and stop at each to settle it before the next:

1. Starting point: who, how experienced per way of working, domain known?
2. Outcome: behaviour, in their own work, learning or performance?
3. Evidence: what trace, and could AI produce it alone?
4. Practice: whole tasks from their own work, with errors to find and a case where no AI is right.
5. Feedback and reflection: debrief, outside feedback for calibration.
6. Transfer and environment: first real use, manager, return after a gap.
7. Evaluation: what shows the change a month later, and who sees it.

Then run the alignment check and the AI stress-test from [cross-layer-checks.md](cross-layer-checks.md).

Give a shape before a schedule. Timings come last, after the outcome and the evidence are agreed.

## Iterating

After a revision, ask what the designer changed and why, and check it against the points raised. Keep a flagged risk on the table until the designer has decided it, in either direction.
